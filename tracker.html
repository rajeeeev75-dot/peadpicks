What is PEAD? (short synopsis)

PEAD is post-earnings announcement drift. After a company reports quarterly results, its share price often keeps moving in the direction of the surprise for weeks or even a few months, instead of adjusting fully on the result day. Good surprises tend to be followed by further gains, bad surprises by further weakness.

Why it happens. Investors and analysts tend to under-react at first. Estimates are revised step by step, large funds build positions gradually, and many people anchor on the old story. The full impact of the news therefore reaches the price slowly.

What is usually looked at. How far profit and sales beat or missed expectations, how the price and volume reacted on the result day, whether the move holds or fades in the days after, whether analysts raise or cut estimates, and whether the stock stays strong relative to its sector and to Nifty.

How to use it. Treat it as a way to shortlist stocks that have just reported and to track whether the post-result move is still working, not as a signal on its own. The effect is usually stronger in smaller, less covered stocks, and costs, thin trading and sudden reversals can remove much of the gain.

Academic basis: Ball and Brown (1968) and Bernard and Thomas (1989). General information, not buy or sell advice.
